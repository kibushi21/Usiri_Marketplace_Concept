3. Five-minute context brief

The project proposes a curated marketplace for home, kitchen and small electrical goods in Dar es Salaam. Verified shops list products; the platform collects payment through a licensed provider, coordinates delivery through a licensed carrier, handles returns and stands behind the order. It is asset-light: no stock, no warehouse, no fleet at pilot.

The model earns an 8% commission (to be tested at 6–12%), a supplier subscription, a delivery coordination fee and, later, sponsored placement. At an assumed TZS 75,000 average order, contribution is about TZS 3,730 per order. A moderate cost structure needs about 1,206 orders a month to break even; the pilot target is 900. At 1,000 orders the moderate structure loses about TZS 770,000 a month.

Usiri is a possible carrier and brand partner. It has not been approached, has not agreed to anything, and publishes no API, rate card or liability terms. The plan is designed to work without it.

5. Decision matrix

Instructions

  1. Agree the weights before scoring. The proposed weights are below.
  2. Each participant scores privately from 1 (very poor) to 5 (very strong).
  3. Discuss the two lowest and two highest scores.
  4. Re-score once, then calculate the weighted average.
  5. Record the result and any dissent.
Criterion Weight Question to ask Score (1–5) Weighted
Commercial attractiveness 25% Is the customer problem large and urgent? Will they pay?
Feasibility 20% Can this team build and operate the MVP?
Cost and funding 15% Is it affordable, with a credible path to break-even?
Risk 20% Is the downside acceptable, including partner dependence?
Strategic fit 10% Does it fit our skills, network and ambitions?
Evidence quality 10% Do we have enough evidence to proceed?
Total 100% /5

Interpretation (agree before scoring):

  • 4.0–5.0: proceed to Phase 2 with confidence.
  • 3.0–3.9: proceed, but fix the weakest criterion first.
  • 2.0–2.9: revise the concept and reassess.
  • Below 2.0: stop or pivot.

Warning: do not score evidence quality highly because the concept document is long. It is desk research. There are no customer, supplier or carrier interviews yet.

6. Discussion questions

Use these to structure the conversation, not to fill time.

Customer

  1. Which specific customer are we serving, and what do they buy today?
  2. Would we use this service ourselves? What would stop us?
  3. What would make a first-time buyer trust us with TZS 75,000?
  4. Is a TZS 5,000–7,000 delivery charge realistic, or must it be absorbed?

Supplier

  1. Which five shops would we approach first, and why would they say yes?
  2. What commission would make them refuse?
  3. Can they pack to a standard, and who trains them?
  4. What happens when a supplier cancels or sends the wrong item?

Partner

  1. What exactly do we want from Usiri: carriage, brand, branches, or all three?
  2. What are we willing to give in return?
  3. What is our walk-away condition?
  4. If Usiri says no, do we still proceed? (The answer should be yes.)

Money and team

  1. How much can the group afford to lose?
  2. Who funds the research phase, and on what terms?
  3. What evidence would justify the next tranche?
  4. Who is accountable for each phase, and what happens if a founder leaves?

7. Usiri preparation checklist

Do not leave the meeting without agreeing who will lead the Usiri conversation and what they may say.

What we may say

  • We are exploring a marketplace concept and would like to understand your parcel services.
  • We are interested in a possible carrier agreement, and possibly a brand licence, but nothing is agreed.
  • We will not use your name publicly without written permission.
  • We would like a written rate card, service level and liability terms.

What we must not say

  • “Usiri is partnering with us.”
  • “We have Usiri’s approval.”
  • “We will guarantee you X parcels.”
  • “We can pay a franchise fee” (before the group has approved it).
  • “We will use your brand” (before a licence is signed).

What to request in writing

  1. Legal entity, registration and TIN.
  2. Routes, transit times and parcel limits.
  3. Rate card, volumetric weight, surcharges and minimums.
  4. Liability limit, claims process and insurance.
  5. Tracking and proof of delivery.
  6. Capacity and peak-season constraints.
  7. Payment and reconciliation terms.
  8. Termination and wind-down.
  9. Data-sharing and PDPA roles.
  10. Brand licence interest and any fee expectation.

Walk-away conditions

  • Exclusive commitment without volume guarantees.
  • Upfront franchise fee before a pilot.
  • Liability cap below parcel value with no insurance.
  • No written service level.
  • Rates revised after onboarding.
  • Multi-year lock-in.
