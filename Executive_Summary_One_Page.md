# Usiri Marketplace — one-page executive summary

**A proposed multi-vendor e-commerce and coordinated-delivery platform for Dar es Salaam**

**Status:** Concept under investigation. Not an approved project. No Usiri partnership, franchise, licence, funding or integration exists. All figures are planning assumptions.

## The idea

A curated marketplace where verified Tanzanian shops sell everyday home, kitchen and small electrical goods to Dar es Salaam households. The platform brings customers, collects payment through a licensed provider, coordinates delivery through a licensed carrier, handles returns and stands behind the order. It does not buy stock, own a warehouse or run a fleet at pilot stage. It earns an 8% commission (to be tested at 6–12%), a supplier subscription, a delivery coordination fee and, later, sponsored placement.

## Why the group sees an opportunity

Mobile money is now mainstream: about 75.78 million active mobile money users and 2.79 million merchants in 2025 [S6]. Internet subscriptions reached 49.3 million by March 2025 [S5]. Yet much online buying still happens through Instagram, WhatsApp and classifieds, where the buyer pays a stranger and delivery is improvised. Jumia left Tanzania in 2019 and had not confirmed a return as of November 2025 [S9][S10]. No locally dominant operator currently combines verified sellers, reliable delivery and buyer protection at scale.

## The honest numbers

At an assumed TZS 75,000 average order, each order contributes about **TZS 3,730** before fixed costs. A moderate structure loses about **TZS 770,000 a month at 1,000 orders**. The business only works at volume, with disciplined fixed costs and ancillary revenue.

| Scenario | Monthly fixed costs | Break-even orders/month | Orders/day | 12-month funding need |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Lean | TZS 2.2m | 295 | 10 | TZS 31.2m |
| Moderate | TZS 5.6m | 1,206 | 40 | TZS 129.8m |
| Ambitious | TZS 12.0m | 2,922 | 97 | TZS 324.9m |

The lean scenario is the only one a student group could plausibly fund from savings. It assumes unpaid founders and a minimal team. The moderate and ambitious scenarios require outside capital and a proven operating playbook.

## The Usiri question

Usiri describes 6+ years, 50+ routes and 100+ agents in transport and parcel carriage [S1][S2]. These are self-reported claims. No API, rate card, liability terms or working tracking page could be found; the tracking link returned HTTP 404 [S4]. Usiri is therefore a **candidate partner for investigation**, not a foundation. A future deal could combine a preferred-carrier agreement, a narrow brand licence and collection-point access, each with separate written terms. The project can proceed under an independent brand with two carriers if Usiri declines.

## Recommendation

**Do not launch. Fund a bounded research phase:** eight weeks, TZS 2–5 million, named owners, and a written go/no-go decision. The research must test whether customers will pay TZS 5,000–7,000 for delivery, whether 15+ suppliers will accept 8%, what carriers actually charge, and whether a brand licence is affordable and non-exclusive. No franchise fee or brand-specific investment should be paid before due diligence.

## What the group must decide

1. Approve, revise, pause or stop the research phase.
2. Set the budget cap and appoint phase owners.
3. Agree the evidence thresholds and stop-loss triggers.
4. Agree founder roles, equity, IP and decision rights in writing.
5. Confirm that the project proceeds on an independent-brand track in parallel with any Usiri discussion.

**Bottom line:** this is a real problem and a hard business. The next step is not to build an app; it is to spend a small amount of money finding out whether customers, suppliers and carriers will actually support the model.
